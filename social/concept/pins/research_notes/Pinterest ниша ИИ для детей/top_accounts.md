# Top Pinterest accounts and boards: "AI + kids + learning" and neighbouring niches (2025-2026)

Method note (read first): pinterest.com cannot be reached from this environment, and most blog sites also failed to load
(DNS blocked: learningliftoff.com, pragmaticmom.com, theeducatorsspinonit.com). All findings below come from web-search
result snippets and the pages those snippets quote. About 18 searches were run. Very few Pinterest numbers could be
verified. Where a number is missing it is listed under Gaps, not estimated. The coordinator should treat this file as a
**partial account list plus a list of what to check by hand on Pinterest** (profile pages show monthly views and
followers directly).

## Which accounts have the largest reach, and which pins went viral?

### Takeaway
The only large account with a verified follower count in the kids-learning neighbourhood is The Educators' Spin On It
(~1.63M followers, January 2024). Search found no verified follower counts for Little Bins for Little Hands, Kids
Activities Blog, Steam Powered Family, Frugal Fun for Boys and Girls, Coffee Cups and Crayons, Hands On As We Grow,
Khan Academy, Common Sense Media, Outschool, Tynker, Code.org or ABCmouse. No pin-level save counts were found for any
account. No dedicated "AI for kids" Pinterest account of any size turned up.

### Inferences
- The reach winners in the neighbourhood are long-running US kids-activity / early-learning blogs (Educators' Spin On It, Pragmatic Mom, likely Kids Activities Blog and Little Bins), not edtech brands. Brand accounts like Scholastic sit near 100K followers, an order of magnitude below the top bloggers.
- The 2026 trend data says "screen-free" and "educational activities" are rising fast. An AI-for-kids account would need a "screen-smart / done together with a parent / ends in something offline" framing to fit this mood, rather than a pure screen-tool framing.

### Gaps

- No pin-level save/repin counts found for any account. Search engines do not index Pinterest save counts in snippets.
- Third-party Pinterest analytics trackers (Social Blade-style) did not appear in results for any of these accounts.

## What content types do they use?

### Takeaway
Evidence points to free printables / downloadable activity packs, listicles ("60 STEM activities"), seasonal packs and
step-by-step activity posts as the core formats of the big kids-learning accounts. For AI-for-teachers content, the
dominant format around the web is the prompt list and prompt infographic. No source measured which format wins on
Pinterest specifically.

### Cited Findings
- Little Bins for Little Hands publishes seasonal free STEM packs as PDFs (Winter STEM Pack, Earth Day STEM Pack for K-2, Summer STEM Pack, Valentine's STEM Pack, LEGO Earth Day Challenges), including screen-free coding printables (printable algorithm game, binary code craft). — [Winter STEM Pack PDF](https://littlebinsforlittlehands.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Winter-STEM-Pack-for-Kids-2019.pdf); [Earth Day pack PDF](https://littlebinsforlittlehands.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Earth-Day-Science-STEM-Pack-Complete.pdf); [homeschoolgiveaways.com linking its free algorithm game](https://homeschoolgiveaways.com/how-to-put-together-inexpensive-stem-ideas-with-free-printable)
- AI-for-teachers formats: long prompt lists (Alice Keeler "100 Prompts for Teachers to Ask ChatGPT", 2023; Class Tech Tips 50 prompts with infographic, 2023) and short infographics (Britannica Education "5 AI Prompts to Help Educators Differentiate Learning"; Moreland University AI prompting do's and don'ts). — [alicekeeler.com](https://alicekeeler.com/2023/03/09/100-prompts-for-teachers-to-ask-chatgpt/); [classtechtips.com](https://classtechtips.com/2023/03/15/chatgpt-prompts-for-teachers/); [elearn.eb.com](https://elearn.eb.com/resource/ai-prompts/); [teach.moreland.edu](https://teach.moreland.edu/ai-prompting-infographic)
- AI-activities-for-kids web content (the material an AI-for-kids pin would link to) is mostly listicles from coding schools: CodaKid, JetLearn, CodeWizardsHQ, plus a parent-tested list on aitoolsforkids.com ("8 We've Actually Done", 2026, with age, time and tool per activity) and Northwestern's printable "AI Unplugged" activities. — [aitoolsforkids.com](https://aitoolsforkids.com/blog/ai-activities-for-kids); [codakid.com](https://codakid.com/blog/ai-for-kids/best-free-ai-activities-homeschoolers/); [jetlearn.com](https://www.jetlearn.com/blog/discover-ai-activities-for-kids-fun-learning-ideas); [codewizardshq.com](https://www.codewizardshq.com/7-educational-ai-activities-for-kids-to-explore/); [AI Unplugged, Northwestern](https://sites.northwestern.edu/aiunplugged)
- AI coloring pages: a crowded seller niche (Etsy/KDP) where guides treat Pinterest as a main traffic source and recommend one pin per coloring page; seasonal peaks (Thanksgiving, Christmas). Sales figures in these guides are unverified seller-blog claims. — [digitalbizplr.com](https://digitalbizplr.com/blogs/learn/how-to-make-coloring-pages-sell-etsy-ai); [colorbliss.com](https://colorbliss.com/blog/ai-coloring); [oakgen.ai](https://oakgen.ai/blog/ai-coloring-book-pages-etsy)

### Inferences
- Gap in the market: AI content on the web for kids is either coding-school listicles or teacher prompt lists. Parent-and-child, hands-on "make something together with AI" pins with a free printable seem rare.
- Copying the Little Bins model (free seasonal pack PDF, then paid club) is the closest proven pattern for a course.

### Gaps
- No data on video pins vs static pins vs idea pins performance in this niche.
- No before/after examples with measured performance found.

## Which link to courses/products and how?

### Takeaway
The clearest documented funnel is Little Bins for Little Hands: free seasonal STEM PDF packs plus a paid members club.
Outschool pushes Pinterest through teacher pin templates. Teacher sellers (TpT) are taught to drive Pinterest traffic
to their stores via podcasts and courses.

### Cited Findings
- Little Bins: free printable packs, plus a paid membership ("Join The Club") giving access to all quick printable activities. — [members.littlebinsforlittlehands.com](https://members.littlebinsforlittlehands.com/?p=2)
- Outschool: Pinterest templates in the teacher handbook, so teachers pin their own classes. — [teach.outschool.com](https://teach.outschool.com/handbook/pinterest-templates/)
- TpT sellers: a 2025 Teacher Biz Podcast episode on using Pinterest to grow digital products; "Terrific Pinning" course for TpT teacher-authors; podcasts about cutting traffic costs for TpT sellers. — [Teacher Biz Podcast ep. 122, 2025](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2183865/episodes/17963783-122-how-to-use-pinterest-to-grow-your-digital-products-business-in-2025-with-nadalie-bardo); [alexandrabaxter.thinkific.com](https://alexandrabaxter.thinkific.com/courses/terrific-pinning); [Terrific Teacherpreneur podcast](https://theterrificteacherpreneur.buzzsprout.com/1552243/14741440)
- Pinterest's own educator guidance (community forum): saves and link clicks matter more than monthly views. — [community.pinterest.biz, Best strategies for educators](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/best-strategies-on-pinterest-for-educators/42531)
- Mom/lifestyle bloggers monetise Pinterest traffic mainly through ad networks (Mediavine) and affiliate links; reported incomes are self-reported/promotional. — [makingsenseofcents.com, Jun 2025](https://www.makingsenseofcents.com/2025/06/how-i-use-pinterest-to-get-blog-traffic-and-make-a-full-time-income.html)

### Inferences
- For a course, the proven shape is: pin -> blog post or free printable (email opt-in) -> paid product.
